## 1. Don’t skip the greeting

Don’t skip the greeting. The cut starts before the clippers do. Skip it, and they sit down guarded. They say less, and you end up cutting a guess.

The order in the lesson: eye contact before the cape, so stop what you’re doing and look up. Then say hello, and use their name. The example in the video: “Hey Mike, good to see you.” The twist: the greeting isn’t small talk. It’s the start of your consultation. Then ask one question (“What are we doing today?”), stop talking, and listen.

**Hello first. Cape second. Clippers last.**

> “Don’t skip the greeting. The cut starts before the clippers do.”  
> — GSWAU short lesson, “Don’t Skip The Greeting”

## 2. Let them finish. The last detail often comes last

Let them finish. Cutting in is how you miss the cut they actually wanted. They say keep it low on the sides, and you’re already reaching for the clippers. You never hear the rest.

Three steps. Hear the whole ask: when they stop talking, count to two. Then say it back: “So, low on the sides, length on top, right?” The twist is that the detail that changes the cut often comes last. Like, *but leave my beard alone.* Then, and not before, pick up the clippers.

**Listen first. Say it back. Then cut.**

> “The detail that changes the cut often comes last.”  
> — GSWAU short lesson, “Let Them Finish”

## 3. You answered too soon

You answered too soon. They were still talking, and you already had the cut picked. That’s how they walk out with a haircut they didn’t ask for.

Let the sentence end. Your plan is just a guess until they stop. Then say it back. The example in the video: “So, shorter on the sides, and it keeps sticking up in the back?” The twist: answering fast feels like confidence. To them, it sounds like you weren’t listening. Then answer. Now your plan is their plan.

> “Answering fast feels like confidence. To them, it sounds like you weren’t listening.”  
> — GSWAU short lesson, “You Answered Too Soon”

## 4. Three words that quietly lose you a client

Three words in your consult quietly lose you a client. They won’t argue. They’ll just smile, pay, and book someone else.

The three phrases, and what the lesson says to say instead:

1. **“What you want?”** It makes them guess. Ask, “What do you like, and what bugged you last time?”
2. **“Got it.”** They said just clean it up, and you nodded. Say it back: “Same length, sharper line, right?”
3. **“Hope you like it.”** Hope isn’t a plan. Before the clippers, say: “Here’s the plan. Sound right?”

The twist: “Got it” sounds polite, and that’s the problem. A nod hides the guess. Ask what they like. Say it back. Get a yes before you cut.

> “A nod hides the guess.”  
> — GSWAU short lesson, “3 Words That Lose You A Client”

## 5. Talk or quiet? Don’t guess

Talk or quiet? Don’t guess. Your client tells you in the first minute. Guess wrong, and they get small talk they never wanted, or a chair that feels cold.

Watch the first minute. How they sit down and answer hello tells you most of what you need. Ask one open question, then listen. A full answer means keep going. A short one means ease off.

The twist: if the phone comes out or their eyes close, you didn’t lose them. Quiet can be the best part of their week. Quiet doesn’t mean cold. Check the mirror with them before you take any length off.

**Watch the first minute. Ask once. Then follow their lead.**

> “Quiet can be the best part of their week.”  
> — GSWAU short lesson, “Client Talk Or Quiet?”

## 6. Don’t fill the quiet with a sale

You filled the quiet. They went silent, and you jumped in with a sale. That pushes them, and the maybe turns into a polite no.

Leave the quiet there. They’re looking in the mirror, deciding. Count two breaths before you say a word. It feels long to you. It doesn’t to them. The twist: quiet isn’t a no. Quiet is where they decide. Then ask one question. Not a pitch, just a question, and let them answer.

**Leave the quiet. Count two breaths. Ask one question.**

> “Quiet isn’t a no. Quiet is where they decide.”  
> — GSWAU short lesson, “You Filled the Quiet”

It’s the same rule as [Retail Products: Extra Income Behind the Chair](/blog/retail-products-extra-income): recommend, don’t push.

## FAQ

**What should I say when a client sits down?**  
Don’t skip the greeting. Eye contact before the cape, hello and their name, then one question: “What are we doing today?” Then stop talking and listen.

**Why does letting them finish matter?**  
The detail that changes the cut often comes last. If you jump in after “keep it low on the sides,” you never hear the rest, like “but leave my beard alone.”

**What if I already know the cut they need?**  
If they’re still talking and you already have the cut picked, they can walk out with a haircut they didn’t ask for. Let the sentence end, say it back, then answer.

**Which phrases should I drop in the consult?**  
The lesson flags three: “What you want?”, “Got it,” and “Hope you like it.” Ask what they like and what bugged them last time, say the plan back, and get a yes before you cut.

**What if the client goes quiet?**  
Quiet can be the best part of their week, and it isn’t automatically a no. Don’t fill it with a sale. Count two breaths, then ask one question, not a pitch.
