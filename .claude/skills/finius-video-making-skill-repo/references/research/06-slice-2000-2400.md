## B. Timing rules (measured)

### B.1 Shot length (41 shots, 1199.5–1444.9; the 0.6 s black slug excluded)
- **Mean 6.1 s, median 5.9 s**, interquartile range 5.0–7.6 s, min 1.5 s (button line from the interviewee), max 11.8 s (pacing-legs office shot under a J-cut).
- Distribution: <4 s: 7 · 4–6 s: 14 · 6–8 s: 11 · ≥8 s: 8.
- This slice is a **slower register** than the "no static shot over 4 s" claim. The rule that actually holds is **no static frame**: every shot of 8 s or more has a continuous move. The 8.9 s map has push plus rotate. The 9.8 s painting has three eased roves. The 8.6 s plaque has an accelerating dolly. The 11.0 / 11.8 s shots are live-action with motion inside the frame. Locked-off stills never exceed about 5 s.
- Longest holds sit at **landmark lines**: the map exposition (8.9 s), the climax plaque (8.6 s), and the strand-switch plaque (8.25 s).

### B.2 Cut vs narration word onset (cut time minus word start; negative = picture first)
Peabody name -160 · quote start -525 · "Junius" -232 · "1867" -125 · "notoriously" -335 · "On July 19" -390 · "aspirations" +43 · "Bismarck always" -250 · "access" -25 · "away" -118 · "While" +113 · "Following" +203 · "1868" -103 · "prestige" -30 · "investing" -57 · map pull-back vs "financing" ±0.
- **Median ≈ -110 ms. 12 of 16 cuts land 0–400 ms before the key word.** The picture shows the thing just before it is named.
- Same-strand content cuts: **-30 to -390 ms**.

  - The Rothschild London plaque appears while the voice still says "Junius Morgan".

### B.3 Text hold vs reading time
- **Only one designed caption in 4 minutes: the author lower third.**
  - Two lines, white, no box, very soft shadow.
  - Line 1 "Author": light/thin sans, cap height ≈ **3.4% H**.
  - Line 2 name: bold geometric/humanist sans (Open Sans Bold–like), all caps, tracking ≈ **+0.12 em**, cap height ≈ **4.8% H**.
  - Left edge x ≈ **10% W**, name baseline ≈ **90% H**.
  - **In**: name slides in from the left edge with motion blur, 1200.83→1201.33 (**0.5 s, expo-out**); "Author" fades/rises **+0.17 s** later over about 0.3 s.
  - **Out** (reverse order): "Author" fades at 1204.67; name slides out left 1204.83→1205.33 (0.5 s, expo-in).

## C. Introducing a rival / second storyline

**How the new major character (Junius Morgan) is introduced:**
1. **Clean slate**: black screen and silence (2.5–3 s) separate him from everything before. He fades up out of black and is never cut in.
2. **The portrait is an object in a room, not a full-frame still.** First look: tiny, dim, framed portrait in a dark Unreal room (lum about 15/255). He is "discovered", not presented.
3. **Then his house, before his face again**: a door with the firm's nameplate. Character equals institution.
4. **Live-action stand-ins** carry the biography. A man's back, then a young man in a top hat. The face of the actor is never tied to the name with a caption.
5. **Authority card**: the photographed hardback of the source biography, with a 2-line "Author / NAME" lower third. This is the only designed lower third. It is used for the **source of a quote**, not for the character.
6. **Second look at the portrait** in a different setting (bright white gallery wall, rack focus blur→sharp). Repeating the same portrait in 3–4 different rooms makes him a recurring "face":
   - 1181: dark room
   - 1207: white gallery
   - 1307–1312: blue parlour, then close
   - 1391: tilted on a desk
   - At his decisive moment (1317) the portrait **comes alive** (photoreal AI face, push to the eyes) over his inner reasoning.
7. **No on-screen name caption for characters.** Names come from the narration. Identification relies on repetition of the same portrait.

**How the antagonist (Bismarck) is introduced:**
- Same grammar, built for menace:
  - his "room" first (study with a chessboard)
  - then the portrait with a **dark predatory foreground occluder** (eagle silhouette)
  - then war paintings
  - then a **real descendant as witness**, using the source documentary's own lower third
  - then the illustration of him pointing.
- About 50 s and 9 shots.

**Colour / material coding of the two houses [I from the frames; consistent across the slice]:**
- **Morgan** = warm brass on black velvet:
  - brass nameplates (Peabody door, J.S. Morgan plaque)
  - dark wood
  - amber key light
  - portrait settings cool-blue only at the "thinking" moment.
- **Rothschild** = gold-and-glass opulence that dims over time:
  - Paris: bright gold frame plus chandelier (lum 126) at the height of their power
  - London (decline section): **black glass with cold silver letters and roaming sparkle**, surround almost black (lum 9–25).
  - The same "house plaque" device, but cold and dark, signals the reversal.
- **War / nations** = saturated symbolic colour:
  - redcoats red
  - French tricolour on blue sky (the only cold, bright, saturated sky)
  - map colour-coded by country.

**How the two strands are cross-cut or compared:**
- **Nameplate rhyme**: each house is "signed" by its plaque. The strand hand-off is plaque (J.S. Morgan, push-in at the climax) → 8.5 s music breath → plaque (Rothschild London).
- **Picture-first switching**: when narration pivots between houses, the image of the other house appears 2–5 s before its name. The viewer is already "in the other building" when the voice turns.
- **Narrative mirror**: the rival's refusal ("no faith in France") is played over the rival's own space: the dark counting-house overhead film plus the enemy's portrait. Then the picture moves to Morgan's room *while* the refusal is still being spoken. Disagreement is shown as two rooms.
- **Scale shift**: Morgan stays in rooms and objects. The rival's new chapter opens on **3D globe maps** (Europe with gold bank houses → Egypt → whip to India). The rival is shown going "global" while Morgan's story stayed intimate.

**How the war is paced (about 1213–1384):**
- Almost no battle footage. Only one live-action war clip (redcoats, 8.9 s, used as a **mood pre-lap** before "wars" is said). The rest is **framed paintings with camera moves**: 6 paintings or prints in 2 minutes. Each painting gets 5–10 s with one dominant move:
  - eased roves
  - defocus→focus pull
  - push.
- Order: map (cause) → statesman's room → statesman portrait → declaration (artillery print) → battlefield (horsemen) → witness → finance plot → **silence + black** → outcome paintings (surrender → treaty → proclamation) → statesman's portrait (vindicated) → Paris street (recovery).
- The war's *outcome* is set off by the only total silence and black frame in the slice. The war's *start* gets only a cut.

## D. Patterns and recipes for our stack (HyperFrames 1920x1080 @ 30 fps, GSAP, AI stills, OmniVoice "Renee", whisper words, SFX library, ffmpeg)

**D1. Story reset after a sponsor or section break**
- Music: `afade=t=out:d=3.3` on the old bed.
- 2.5 s of full black: `#000` scene, 75 frames.
- New bed starts **0.6 s before** the first word; picture fades in from black over **1.5 s** (`opacity 0→1, ease:"power1.inOut"`), starting about 0.9 s before the first word.
- Target -46 LUFS in the gap (essentially silent).

**D2. Character as framed object (portrait-in-room)**
- Generate one portrait still per major character. Reuse it in 3–5 different AI room plates (dark panelled, white gallery, blue parlour, desk-top tilted 8–12°).
- Composite in HyperFrames:
  - room plate as background
  - portrait inside a frame layer
  - `filter: blur(14px→0)` over 1.8 s for a rack-focus "discovery"
  - plus a dolly: `scale 1.00→1.08` over the shot (`ease:"none"` or `"sine.inOut"`)
  - optional glass highlight band (white, 8% opacity, slanted gradient).
- First appearance: lum ≈ 6–8% (very dark), filling about 20% of the frame width.
- Decisive moment: crossfade (1.0 s) to an image-to-video "alive" close-up of the same face; push `scale 1→1.6` over 5 s toward the eyes.

**D3. House ident = nameplate**
- Each faction or brand gets a diegetic plaque: brass on black velvet for the hero, black glass plus silver for the rival.
- Climax push: `scale 0.12→1.0` and `rotation -10°→-4°` over **8.5 s**, `ease:"power2.in"` (accelerating). Let the strand's final line land at about 60% of the move.
- Rival plaque: slower push `1.0→1.12`. Add 2–3 animated specular glints (radial-gradient white dots, `mix-blend-mode:screen`, travelling 300 px in 1.2 s, opacity 0→0.9→0).

**D4. Strand hand-off**
- Last line of strand A → **music alone 8.5 s** (duck the bed back up to -21 LUFS, about 3.5 dB under the narration level).
- Plaque close-up then a neutral establishing shot (5.5 s).
- Music decays about 10 dB, then a **low riser / sub swell 1.5 s** peaking on the cut to strand B's ident.
- First narration word of strand B **+0–120 ms after** the cut.
- Strand B's image appears while the voice may still mention A.

**D5. Picture-first cut rule (automate from whisper words)**
- Same-subject cut: `cut = keyword.start - 0.11 s` (allowed range -0.40…+0.05).
- New-subject or new-place cut: `cut = firstMention.start - 2.0…4.5 s`, placed in the nearest word gap ≥ 0.25 s.
- Quotes: cut to the "source" image 0.5 s before the quote starts.

**D6. Source / author lower third** (the only designed caption type here)
- Two lines, left-aligned at x = 10% W, name baseline at 90% H.
- Line 1 (role): light weight, cap 3.4% H (≈ 37 px), white 90%.
- Line 2 (name): bold, all caps, cap 4.8% H (≈ 52 px), letter-spacing 0.12 em, white, `text-shadow 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,.35)`.
- In:
  - name `x:-60%→0`, `filter: blur(12px)→0` over 0.5 s, `ease:"expo.out"`
  - role `opacity 0→1, y:+8→0` over 0.3 s at +0.17 s.
- Timing: starts 1.3 s after the cut, holds 3.3 s, out in reverse order (role fades, then name `expo.in` to the left over 0.5 s), clear ≥ 1.5 s before the next cut.
- Use only for real sources we are allowed to cite.

**D7. Painting rove (for archival art and AI "paintings")**
- Present the image as a framed object (gilt frame PNG, wallpaper plate) or full-bleed with a canvas-weave overlay (`multiply` at 25–35%).
- In a 10 s shot, do 3 keyframed moves of 3.8 / 1.3 / 1.8 s with `ease:"sine.inOut"` and 0.5–0.9 s holds between them. Each move covers 8–15% of the frame, ending on the detail the narration names next.
- Alternative opener: `blur 20px→0` over 2.4 s, then push `1.0→1.06`.

**D8. Act-break silence**
- Before a major outcome: end narration on a bright, literal image.
- Then 2.5–3.0 s of **near-silence** (bed muted, -37 LUFS room tone, about 20 dB under the narration level) while the image holds.
- Then **0.6 s black**.
- Then a hard cut to the outcome image with a **sub drone swelling +30–37 dB over 2.6 s**. The first word lands at the swell's peak.
- Use about once per 10 minutes.

**D9. War without combat footage**
- One short live-action or AI motion clip as a mood pre-lap (5–9 s, placed in a 2–3 s narration pause, music alone 8 dB under the narration level).
- Then 5–7 paintings or prints at 5–10 s each, each with a single move.
- Order: cause map → aggressor portrait → declaration → battlefield → witness → outcome.

**D10. 2.5D political map**
- Flat colour per country (hero blue, rivals purple, neutrals orange, green or tan, rest grey 40% luminance), pale sea, paper texture, vignette.
- Static Trajan-like caps labels at 2.5% H, 70% white.
- Move: `scale 1→1.05` plus `rotation 0→3°` over 9 s, linear.
- Cut in 100–150 ms before the first date or place word.

**D11. 3D globe hops (expansion beats)**
- Use real satellite textures in a three.js scene or pre-rendered plates.
- Place one flag on a pole (image-to-video or a sine-wave cloth shader).
- Label in a script face, about 4% H, 35% opacity, or glowing gold blackletter for "houses".
- Hop between places with a **0.25 s motion-blur whip** (directional blur 40 px plus a whoosh SFX at -18 dB, peaking on the blur frame).
- End each place with a 1.1 s `power2.out` pull-back timed to its "and …" word.

**D12. Witness interviews**
- J-cut the witness voice 1.5–3.8 s before the face.
- Keep the face shots ≤ 11 s and intercut 4–5 s illustrations.
- Duck music to -30 dB under the voice or remove it.
- End on a 1.5 s button shot of the witness before the narration returns 1.9 s later over a new image.
- (In our videos the witness is replaced by an on-screen quote card or a host clip; same timing.)
