# Slice 08: 28:00–32:00 (1680–1920 s), sheets 056–063

Chapters in this slice (from meta.json): end of "Bailing Out The Federal Reserve" (–1704), **"Road to War — Europe on The Brink" 1704–1801**, **"World War I" 1801–1881**, **"The Decline of The Rothschild Dynasty" 1881–1945**.

How I measured: cuts from work/cuts.txt (scdet thr 10). I also found transitions it missed (whip-pans, a zoom-blur, a punch-in) by sampling frames at 10 fps. I extracted 24 fps grey frames at 160x90 for luma, saturation, sharpness and motion. Camera scale and drift per shot come from a brute-force scale+shift fit between the first and last frame. Treat it as rough when the fit error is over 30, which happens when the subject moves; I mark that as "(fit noisy)". Audio is work/audio.wav, with RMS and a low/mid ratio (<120 Hz vs 300–3000 Hz) at 0.1 s steps. A low/mid ratio of +8 dB or more with no speech = a sub boom/hit. Word times are from the VTT. **Caveat:** the first word of every VTT cue carries the cue start time, which can be up to 4 s late after a pause (e.g. "public" is stamped 1692.00 but is spoken at about 1688.3). Wherever a cue-initial word mattered, I re-checked the onset against audio energy.

Source-type key: FILM = feature-film/drama footage (B&W or sepia); ARCH = archive newsreel/photo; COLZ = colourised archive; 3D = Unreal-style rendered room/map; AI = likely AI still/video (skylines, aerials); HOST = creator on camera; INT = borrowed expert interview; GFX = map/graphic.

Rule: the median cut lands **0–300 ms before the phrase-initial word**. The cut goes on the phrase boundary (the start of a clause), not on the noun. The visual noun usually arrives 0.3–1.2 s into the shot ("trench" comes 1.2 s after the trench cut; "war bonds" 0.75 s after the poster cut). Exceptions:
- **J-cuts for on-camera people.** The host's voice leads his picture by ≈1.5 s. The historian's voice leads her picture by ≈2.4 s.
- **Reveal delays for a villain or key face**, about +0.9 s.
- **Long leads before a section reset.** The map cut comes 3.5 s before "After World War I", and the 3D desk cut comes 2.0 s before Natty's name.

**Text hold vs reading time.**
- "JUNE 28, 1914": 12 characters, 3 words, on screen 4.8 s with a ≈1.0 s fade-in. Reading needs about 0.6 s, so it holds **≈8× the reading time**. That is because it is the only information on screen during a 14 s VO gap, and it works as a title card.
- Lower third: 2 lines, 4 words, about 4 s (≈2.5× the reading time). It enters about 2 s after the interviewee's first picture frame (≈4.4 s after her voice starts) and leaves before her second clip.
- All other text in this slice is diegetic (signs, plaques, newspaper, poster, map labels). There are **no narration captions, no kinetic type and no stat cards**, even on "10 million soldiers and 7 million civilians dead".

**Music vs story beats** (RMS: VO peaks −14…−17 dB, bed alone during pauses −22…−30 dB, so the bed sits about 10–13 dB under the VO):

## C. Escalation and decline

**1. Road to War (1704–1766): build by layering, not by speed.**
- The chapter opens with motion: a whip-pan from the US map to the Europe map, then a zoom-blur into archive footage.
- Then comes a steady 2–5 s march of archive: crowd → factory → parade → animated alliance map → soldiers → generals → marching columns. Every cut is frame-accurate to a clause ("fueled", "military buildups", "alliances"), which gives a list-like drumbeat.
- At the turn ("the House of Rothschild sees an opportunity"), the visuals leave the noisy B&W archive for the **dark, warm 3D "family" rooms**: framed family tree → desk photo → lobby → plaque. The picture goes almost static (motion 0.35–2.5 vs 9–15 for the archive). Luma drops from about 90 to 23–45.
- The **sub boom + 1.2 s silence** on "tensions" works as an act button.
- The chapter ends with a foreshadowing line ("more brutal and deadly than any they have ever encountered") over a slow rack-focus onto the "& Sons, London" plaque. Music runs on 1.6 s past the line, then a low hit lands on the cut to the date card.
- The tension device here is **contrast of texture** (frantic archive vs still, dark interiors) plus **one boom per beat**, not a riser. I found no measurable riser. Pace stays at 13–15 cuts/min.

**2. The assassination (1766.8–1779): weight through silence.**
- The narrator steps out for **13.9 s**. A date card ("JUNE 28, 1914") is the only text.
- Feature-film footage plays as if in real time: car arrival → assassin approaches → close-up of the victim → shot fired → struggle → aftermath.
- The music is **faded out over about 3 s (−17 → −31 dB)**, leaving near-silence and diegetic sound. One modest gunshot (≈10 dB over the bed) lands at 1775.9.
- The editing accelerates only inside the event: shots of 6.7 s → 1.6 s → 0.7 → 0.7 → 0.1 → 0.6 → 0.7 s (30 cuts/min), with whip-fast handheld moves (25–40 %/s scale changes).
- Then the release: 1.9 s of dead silence over a wide, bright aftermath shot.
- The narrator returns on a **slow pull-out (−3.7 %/s) over the dead couple**. The pull-out is the "held shot for weight": slow, outward and 3.7 s long.
- **No slow motion** is used. The weight comes from silence, the date card and the outward move.

**3. World War I (1801–1881): scale through literal images, then a pivot to the family.**
- The war itself takes only about 17 s of visuals (trench → trucks → marching → dead soldier). Cuts run at 21–27 per minute, mostly colourised archive in murky green-brown.
- The casualty number is **not graphed or written**. The figure lands on one dead body (shot 42), followed by a 0.9 s VO pause.
- The war is quickly reframed as a family story: two castles = "opposing sides"; a callback to the Natty desk photo; château fountains; then a **fade-up from black on the word "overshadowed"** into a darkened dining room. This visual pun signals the shift from war to decline.

**4. The decline (1858–1881, then the chapter from 1881): shown as a family scene and a cold map.**
- The downfall is acted out with **borrowed drama**: a 1940s B&W family dinner in a locked-off shot/reverse-shot (7 shots, 15 s, no camera moves at all, saturation 0). The stillness itself reads as stagnation.
- Then **total silence for 1.3 s**, followed by a music-only swell over the network map that once showed their rise. Here it is pushed in close on Frankfurt/Vienna as narration says the house is "in disarray". The rise map returns as a callback that now means "scattered".
- Finally the lobby callback runs 7.5 s, slow.
- After that the Germany sequence produces the **fastest pace of the slice** (1.7 s median, 39 cuts/min at 1900–1920). It is all high-contrast B&W archive (saturation about 10). The cut to the first Hitler image is delayed about 0.9 s, there is a 2.3 s held pause before "Chancellor" filled with a symmetrical rally wide, and the word itself lands on an extreme close-up.
- So the **decline is slow and still; the threat that follows is fast**.

**Grade.** There is no global darkening across the chapters. Brightness follows the source (10 s averages swing from 20 to 117). Colour, though, does track the story:
- saturated colour at the American peak (flag, maps, saturation 30–40)
- colourised mud tones for the war
- pure B&W (saturation 0–12) for the decline and the Nazi rise from 1858 on

## D. Patterns and recipes (HyperFrames + our stack)

**R1. Clause-locked cut grid.** Take whisper word times and split the narration into clauses (at commas and conjunctions such as "fueled by", "and", "while"). Place each cut at `clause_start − 0.15 s` (allowed range −0.3…0 s). Shots run 2.0–4.5 s. If a clause is longer than 5 s, split it on its second noun. In the composition, each scene is a sub-comp with `data-start = cut` and `data-duration = next_cut − cut`.

**R2. Mandatory micro-move on every still.** Any still or 3D render held 2 s or more gets a GSAP move:
- **Push:** `scale 1.00→1.03` per second of hold, clamped to a total of 1.12. Use `ease: "none"` or `"sine.inOut"`.
- **Truck:** `x: 0→−(20 px × seconds)`.
- **Choosing the direction:** use a pull-out (−3…−4 %/s) only for weight/aftermath shots and a push-in for everything else. Overscan stills to 115 % so the moves never show an edge.

**R3. Map-to-map whip and zoom-blur chapter entry.**
- Outgoing map ends with a **whip**: 0.5 s, `x: 0→−1920`, `filter: blur(0→40px)`, `ease: "power2.in"`.
- The incoming map starts at `x: +1920→0`, `blur 40→0`, 0.4 s, `ease: "power2.out"`.
- Then push in 3 %/s.
- Exit into the first archive clip with a **zoom-blur**: 0.4 s, `scale 1→1.6` + `blur 0→30px` + `opacity` crossfade 0.15 s. Use a whoosh SFX at −14 dBFS starting 80 ms before the midpoint.

**R4. Sub-boom act button.**
- On the last stressed word of an act: an impact/sub-boom SFX (energy under 120 Hz), peak about −12 dBFS, so it reads about +14 dB in the low band, with a 1.5–2 s tail. Place it 0.1–0.8 s after the word ends.
- Duck the music to −∞ over 0.3 s under the tail.
- Leave **1.0–1.5 s of silence**, then cut to a dark, still establishing shot and start the next VO line 1.5–2 s after the boom.
- Limit: no more than about one per 60–90 s (3 in 240 s here).

**R5. Silent set-piece (the "event" sequence).** Use this for the single most important event of an episode:
- Pull the narrator for 10–15 s.
- Fade the music from the bed level to silence over 3 s.
- Show a date or place card: thin light sans in small caps, pale accent colour, centred, cap height 9–10 % of frame height, fade in 1.0 s, hold 4–5 s, out in 0.2 s.
- Cut the event in an accelerating run: 6 s → 1.6 → 0.7 → 0.7 → 0.6 s, with one diegetic hit SFX only about 10 dB over the room tone.
- Leave 1.5–2 s of dead silence on a wide aftermath shot.
- Narration returns over a 3–4 s **pull-out**.

Our stack: AI image-to-video clips (4–6 s each) or Ken Burns stills with fast GSAP handheld shake (`x/y ±12 px`, 6 Hz noise) for the 0.6–0.7 s shots.

**R6. J-cut expert/host inserts.** When a second voice (a guest, Jordan, a quoted person) enters:
- Start their audio 1.5–2.5 s before their picture, over the outgoing B-roll.
- Then show them for 5–8 s, with a punch-in to close-up (scale ×1.5, hard cut) halfway through.
- Run their audio on over 1–2 cutaways (photo, document).
- Lower third, bottom-left at x = 10 % W, baseline at 90 % H: role in light caps at 3 % H, name in bold caps at 4.2 % H, white, no plate. Bring it in 2 s after their first frame, hold 4 s, remove it before the shot ends.

**R7. Music-only breath at a section reset.** At a chapter or topic change: true silence of 1.0–1.5 s, then the music alone for 3–4 s (bed raised to −20 dB) over a slow push on a callback visual (a map or set seen earlier in the episode), then VO.

Elsewhere, put 2–3 s music-only pauses before reveal words (a name, a title, a number), filled with 1–2 quick shots (0.8–1.0 s) that land the reveal word on a close-up.

**R8. Callback sets.** Build 2–3 persistent "home" scenes as HyperFrames sub-comps and reuse them, each time with a different move or focus:
- a dark desk with a framed photo
- a warm lobby
- a satellite network map

Here the desk is reused 3×, the lobby 2× and the map 3× within 4 minutes. Each return now carries the story's state: on the map, the push-in close on scattered nodes = "disarray". In our stack: one HTML scene per set with parametrised camera (`scale`, `x`, `blur` for rack focus).

**R9. Literal-noun + metaphor alternation.**
- About 70 % of shots illustrate the clause's concrete noun (trench, trucks, poster, newspaper).
- About 30 % are metaphor/symbol at turning points: two castles = opposing sides; a fade-up from black on "overshadowed"; a family dinner = the heir leaving the business.
- Put the metaphor shot on the abstract clause.
- Implement "overshadowed" and similar words as a `opacity 0→1` / `brightness(0.1→1)` fade-up over 1.0 s at the start of the shot.

**R10. Pace curve for an escalation.** Plan cuts/min per 20 s window. Set-up 12–15, event 30, aftermath/explanation 12–20, villain rise 35–40. Before each fast block, put at least one shot of 6 s or more (a held still or interview). The fast run feels fast because of the slow shot before it.

**R11. Colour arc by era/mood.** Grade our stills via ffmpeg `eq=saturation=` or CSS `filter: saturate()`:
- prosperity: saturation 1.0–1.2
- war: 0.6 with a warm-brown tint (`sepia(0.3)`)
- decline/menace: 0 (pure B&W, contrast 1.2)

Keep brightness source-driven; do **not** darken globally.

**R12. Numbers in narration stay unvisualised in tragic contexts.** Death tolls get one human image plus a 0.8–1.0 s VO pause, not a stat card. (Measured here: no text at all on "10 million… 7 million".)

Measured vs inferred:
- **Measured:** cut times, shot lengths, VO onsets, silences, boom positions, luminance/saturation, motion rates, text on/off times.
- **Inferred:** SFX identities beyond the boom and gunshot (whoosh on the whip), the sources (AI vs stock for the aerials and skylines), font families, and the exact fade curves.
